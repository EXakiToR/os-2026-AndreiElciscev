Observe - What exactly is saved and loaded? Why does ra resume the other thread?

thread_switch(old, new) stores 14 64-bit registers in the old thread's context:
ra, sp, and s0 through s11. It loads those 14 registers from the new context.
The saved context occupies 112 bytes. Its offsets are 0 for ra, 8 for sp, and
16 through 104 for s0 through s11. Loading sp selects the other thread's stack.

The call to thread_switch leaves a return address in ra. When the thread is
switched away, that return address is saved in its context. A later switch
restores it, and ret jumps to that address, continuing just after that thread's
earlier call to thread_switch. For a new thread, thread_create initially puts
the worker function's address in ctx.ra and the 16-byte-aligned top of its
8192-byte stack in ctx.sp, so its first ret enters the worker function.

The s registers are callee-saved by the RISC-V calling convention. The compiler
handles caller-saved registers across the ordinary function call boundary.
The switch also retains the return address and stack pointer needed to resume.
All user threads run cooperatively in one xv6 process and share its address space.
They have separate stacks and saved contexts. Switching is a user-space call,
with no additional kernel thread creation.

Implementation
- Context has ra, sp, s0..s11 in the assembly order.
- thread_create initializes ra/sp and marks a slot runnable.
- thread_schedule selects the next runnable slot, updates state/current_thread,
  and calls thread_switch with the old and new context addresses.
- thread_b and thread_c mirror thread_a with their own flags and counters.
- The Makefile includes _uthread and links uthread_switch.o explicitly.
- A boundary check rejects creation if the four-slot table is full.
